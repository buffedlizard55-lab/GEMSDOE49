What this is

The DOE GEMS Prize asks for faults indicative of geothermal resources that are not in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue — and it masks the catalogue when scoring, so mass emitted on known faults is pure cost. This submission scores each pixel by the cross-gradient operator: for the isostatic gravity anomaly, reduced-to-pole magnetics and the detrended elevation surface (all smoothed to a common 300 m scale), it takes the pairwise cross products of the horizontal gradient vectors and keeps pixels where at least 2-of-3 pairs are aligned and all three gradient magnitudes are large (conjunction operator, not a product of score maps). A fault juxtaposing rocks of different density and susceptibility puts both edges on one trace and lights up; a buried volcanic edge that moves only magnetics is suppressed by the magnitude conjunction. Orthogonality is kept as a separate confounder flag, never as evidence.

Metric-aware placement: 40,000 dots via non-maximum suppression (200 m spacing), budget calibrated on the holdout — DTI rose monotonically from 10k→40k dots (0.0155→0.0203), so the break-even bar is not yet reached at 40k; we stop there because family live evidence says over-emission above ~44–46k px earns nothing extra.

Why did GEMSDOE32's h33-2-b2 score the family best (0.2778) — and the path past it

Why it scored: the DTI is a mass budget, DTI = T / (0.2(T+S−M) + 0.8|G|). h33-2-b2 took the 0.2708 scored base and deleted every dot within 200 m of the catalogue (37,654 dots remain, 0 on-catalogue). Deleting mass that can never earn credit (catalogue is masked) while keeping geometry raises credit per dot above the break-even bar (~0.052 credit/dot). That prune, not new detection, is what moved 0.2708 → 0.2778.

How to beat 0.2778 / the 0.3195 leader (PhD-level answer): the leader needs ≈25 % more mean credit per emitted pixel at matched mass than our best field delivers. No public catalogue can supply that — the newest public compilation is already inside the given catalogue. The only two levers are (i) credit density: emit where genuinely new structure is most likely (coupled multi-physics edges off-catalogue — this submission's lever), and (ii) the Final-Round objective: the expanded label set is built from expert review of all submissions, so physically defensible, well-localised candidates score twice — once now, once when experts confirm them. Contrarian value lies in signal channels the catalogue compilers never had: radioelement alteration chemistry (H49-B), en-echelon stepover transfer faults (H49-D), MT conductance corridors (H49-E) — ranked on the hypotheses page.
