Why the highest-scoring artifact in this cohort scored what it did

The brief asks for a PhD-level answer to a specific question: the sibling artifact h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e from the GEMSDOE32 project carries a reported 0.2778, the highest local reading in the cohort reviewed here. What put it there, and where does the remaining distance to the leaderboard's 0.3774 live?

What the artifact actually is

Read from the file itself in this checkout (rasterio): 37,654 positive pixels, exactly two unique values, zero positive pixels on the catalogue and a mean value of 0.0 there. Its own audit JSON describes it as "0.2708 base with every dot at d(catalogue) ≤ 2 px deleted (37,654 dots)" and reports a base lineage from a multiline detector thinned with dot_thin(..., 2.8 px). Two independent facts follow: the file is a sparse binary dot field, and it is deliberately absent from the catalogue and its 200 m neighbourhood.

The three mechanisms that put it at 0.2778

  1. It spends its mass where credit is possible. The metric is a coverage problem under a budget: every unit of prediction mass either lands within 300 m of a new-fault pixel (earning k(d)) or pays 0.2. The catalogue is masked, so a dot there is dead mass. Pruning is not cosmetic — it is the single cheapest DTI gain available, and this session measured the same effect independently: the shipped placement gains +0.0102 collared and +0.0070 unrestricted over the best single-layer baseline when both arms are given the identical rule (scripts/margin_check.py), and the artifact's own project claims +0.004870 in 4/4 folds from the same move.
  2. It is binary and sparse. Because α + β = 1, a graded prediction is dominated by its own thresholded support (proved in tests/test_metric.py). Binary dots also dodge the partial-mass false-positive term that a calibrated probability map pays on every diffuse pixel. Sparse emission is not a stylistic choice here; it is the metric's preference.
  3. It inherited a good carrier. The base is a multiline field built from the topographic / potential-field family, i.e. the same class of evidence that this session's screen ranks highest (topo_ms_coh 0.1074 collared at matched geometry versus the no-information controls 0.0718 random and 0.0693 lattice). Placement cannot manufacture signal: it can only stop wasting it.

Why it is nevertheless rank 13, not rank 1

Its carrier is a lineament detector: it predicts where known-style structures run. The scored truth is the set of faults that the published catalogue is missing, so the ceiling of any catalogue-shaped prediction is the fraction of new truth that happens to follow already-mapped strikes. The board says the gap is real and large: 0.3774 at the top versus 0.2778 here — 0.0996 of headroom which, under the marginal algebra, is worth roughly 500 km of extra correctly placed trace. Nothing in the placement layer closes that; only carrier quality does.

What this implies for a higher-scoring submission

  • Keep the off-catalogue emphasis and the sparse binary form. Both are measured, both are cheap, and both are what the top local artifact got right.
  • Spend the effort on the carrier. The measured limitation here is resolution: every supplied potential field decorrelates over 2.7–8.1 km (docs/data/native_resolution.json), so a 100 m gradient of them is interpolation structure. The one supplied field with grid-scale power is the detrended-elevation slope — which is why it is the best single-layer baseline and why the shipped carrier is built from it.
  • The named next lever is a finer elevation surface. USGS 3DEP best-available (1 m) is free and official (prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com) but is terabytes and unreachable from this environment (HTTP 000, measured). It is the one change that attacks carrier quality rather than placement.
  • Do not chase the coupling as a score. It was implemented exactly as the brief describes and lands inside the control band; its defensible use is the gate shipped here, which is a measured no-op and is labelled as one.
