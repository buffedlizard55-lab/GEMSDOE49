Native resolution — why the naive cross-gradient fails

A 100 m horizontal gradient of a field whose integral scale is 2.7–8.1 km measures the interpolation between regional samples, not a contact. The pure cross-gradient of isostatic gravity and reduced-to-pole magnetics is therefore dominated by interpolation texture — which is exactly what the controls show. The one field with genuine grid-scale power is the detrended-elevation slope (ρ₁ ≈ 0.81), and it is the strongest single-layer baseline. This is the measured answer to the brief's instruction to check the native station spacing before trusting 100 m gradients.

Instrument warning — a holdout win is not a leaderboard win

Rank correlation between candidates' holdout scores and the ~12 live leaderboard scores available locally is positive but weak (≈ 0.50 drift-corrected), and the "hidden catalogue" probe is broken (its random trace scores 0.97, so it is aborted above 0.25). No local instrument significantly ranks candidates the way the private scorer does. Consequences for how this repository behaves:

  • a holdout win is a screen, not evidence of a leaderboard gain;
  • candidates are spread across genuinely different hypotheses rather than micro-tuned;
  • the released file is the one that cleared both gates, with the margin printed above rather than a claimed leaderboard position.

Format gate

scripts/validate_submission.py runs 13 checks on the actual bytes about to be submitted and prints a JSON summary. The two rejection modes observed live are reproduced as tests (tests/test_gate.py) rather than described:

  • mode A — a NaN strictly inside the footprint: legal at the edges, fatal to a naive min/max form check;
  • mode B — a finite value outside [0,1]: a z-score or an unclipped sentinel leaking into the array.

Both variants of the released file are emitted and both pass the gate; the NaN variant echoes the official template's nodata mask exactly, the zero variant is for forms that reject NaN.
