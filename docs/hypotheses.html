Hypothesis backlog — five candidates not previously implemented in GEMSDOE1–48

Each card names the layers, the physical signature, why it targets a catalogue gap rather than a known fault, and how it differs from everything already implemented in the family. Ranked by expected DTI improvement ÷ implementation cost. Negative results stay in the table.

Gap logic used by every card. The QFFD/INGENIOUS catalogue is defined by demonstrable coseismic surface deformation in the past 1.6 Ma, compiled from published detailed-scale studies (CG-1, CG-2 in the family knowledge base). Anything that leaves no surface rupture, or whose evidence post-dates the compilation literature, or whose evidence lives in a channel the compilers never consulted, is structurally incapable of being in the catalogue. The scorer masks the catalogue, so those gaps are the only place credit exists.

1. H49-A — Cross-gradient multi-physics coupling (IMPLEMENTED, THIS SUBMISSION)

  • Layers: band 13 isostatic gravity anomaly, band 2 RTP magnetics, band 12 detrended elevation (common 300 m scale).
  • Signature: pairwise cross-product of horizontal gradient vectors vanishing (Gallardo & Meju 2004 operator) with large magnitudes in all three fields; 2-of-3 consensus via order statistic, not a product of scores.
  • Why a gap: catalogue compilation used topography and field geology; a density+susceptibility juxtaposition expressed coherently in gravity AND magnetics AND topography off-catalogue is exactly the class-3 (cover-masked) / class-1 (correction) material the compilers lacked instruments to chase.
  • Difference: never implemented in GEMSDOE1–48 — the family's multi-physics files (h27-4, d2.8, H32-d) combined score maps; this applies the coupling at the operator level on raw gradients.
  • Status: VALIDATED — holdout DTI 0.0203, +30 % over all single-layer baselines; shipped as this session's submission.

2. H49-B — Radiometric alteration halo × coupled edge

  • Layers: GeoDAWN radiometric K/Th (bridged radiometric_u8.tif, source DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ, CC0) × H49-A coupled score.
  • Signature: robust z-score of log(Th/K) deviating from the local background, locked to a coupled edge — K is mobile in hydrothermal fluids (adularia/illite addition or acid leaching), Th is comparatively immobile.
  • Why a gap: alteration chemically softens rock and erases scarps — altered segments are precisely the ones a geomorphology-driven compilation misses; the radioelement channel is one the compilers never consulted.
  • Difference: 15GEMSDOE (tso1, 0.0782) used alteration-vs-magnetic conjunction without the cross-gradient operator or Th/K z-score; GEMSDOE46 used scarp×radiometric concordance unscored. Nothing gated coupling by alteration.
  • Status: TESTED, REJECTED AS GATE (2026-10-06) — on the hide-and-recover holdout, gating costs DTI at every threshold: z≥1.0 → 0.0179, z≥1.5 → 0.0139, z≥2.0 → 0.0102 vs coupled-only 0.0203 (evidence/h49b_validation.json). Caveat: holdout truth is catalogue-shaped; if live expert labels include altered-hidden systems the channel may still matter for the Final Round. No submission slot will be spent on it until it beats 0.0203.

3. H49-D — En-echelon scarp-pair stepovers (transfer faults)

  • Layers: 1 m lidar scarp matched-filter field (lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif, already on disk) + catalogue orientation field.
  • Signature: pairs of overlapping sub-parallel scarp segments with ≤ ~500 m lateral offset and along-strike separation ≤ 1 km; emit on the connecting transfer structure inside the step-over, not on the segments themselves.
  • Why a gap: compilers map continuous traces; transfer faults inside step-overs are short, oblique and often buried — but Giddens & Faulds (2025, GeoDAWN lidar, ~499,178 km²) document step-overs as fault-dense settings (91.9 % on range fronts). Published, inside this province.
  • Difference: GEMSDOE33's h33d (0.2632, unscored) placed dots from catalogue analogs of tips/step-overs on the d2.8 base; this detects scarp pairs directly from the lidar field, independent of catalogue geometry.
  • Status: READY TO TEST — data on disk; pair-detection script is next session's first action.

4. H49-E — MT conductance corridors across basin floors

  • Layers: band 17 conductivity surface, band 15 depth-to-basement (basin mask), band 2 RTP (as a veto: require no magnetic edge, isolating blind structures).
  • Signature: narrow conductive lineaments (Radon/Hough on conductance at 500 m) crossing thick basin fill, with no magnetic counterpart — clay/fluid-filled blind structures.
  • Why a gap: blind structures with no surface rupture are out of scope of the QFFD by definition (CG-1); MT sees them. The about page itself says many targets are “hidden below the surface, requiring geophysical data to detect”.
  • Difference: family card H6 reserved conductivity as “corroboration only” and was never tested; this tests it as a detector with a magnetic veto, restricted to basin floors.
  • Status: READY TO TEST — all layers already in training_features.tif.

5. H49-C — Strain-rate ridge coherence (“kinematic ghost lines”)

  • Layers: band 4 second invariant + band 7 shear rate (Hessian ridge analysis at ~1 km); catalogue distance mask ≥ 300 m.
  • Signature: narrow ridges of strain-rate invariant whose orientation is coherent with the local catalogue strike field, but offset from every mapped trace.
  • Why a gap: geodetic strain integrates deformation on all structures, including buried strands; the catalogue only contains where rupture was mapped (Blewitt et al. GRC — geothermal correlates with strain accumulation on favorably oriented faults).
  • Difference: family H4 used quantile bins of raw bands as a prior; ridge extraction with orientation coherence as a detector is new.
  • Status: HIGH RISK — the bands are 99.8 % smooth above the 300 m kernel (family measurement); localisation may be impossible. Ranked last of the viable three.
