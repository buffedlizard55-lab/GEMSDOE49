# H49-XG holdout result — 2026-10-06

## Executive decision

**The fixed, preregistered 1 km-class primary screen did not pass its promotion gate. No candidate submission GeoTIFF was generated, no prior file was copied, and no DrivenData slot was used.**

At the primary scale (Gaussian σ = 500 m; FWHM = 1.177 km), the cross-gradient score improved the pooled public-catalogue hide-and-recover DTI over the strongest single-layer baseline by **+0.018475**. It beat the strongest single-layer result in **4/5** folds and both controls, but one fold was **−0.011802**, just beyond the preregistered catastrophic-fold limit of −0.01. Therefore the promotion gate is correctly **FAIL**. A pooled improvement does not override the predeclared fold-stability rule.

The 2 km-FWHM sensitivity had positive deltas in 5/5 folds and passed the *numerical* thresholds when evaluated on the same locked spatial blocks. It remains **sensitivity-only**, not promotion-eligible: the preregistration fixed the 1 km-class scale as primary and did not authorize choosing among scales using the same holdout. Selecting it now would be post-hoc model selection. It needs a newly frozen, independent spatial validation before any submission use.

Neither holdout DTI is a leaderboard score or a reliable numerical estimate of private-test performance. The label source is the public existing-fault catalogue, and the feature files are pinned owner-hosted mirrors rather than organizer-authenticated downloads.

## Spatial-fold audit caveat

The preregistration called for whole-segment folds. The implementation is five buffered east–west raster blocks; the available mirror provides a binary raster, not authoritative fault-segment IDs. A post-run connectivity diagnostic of the label raster found 3,199 8-connected components; 21 components (850 positive pixels inside scored cores) have pixels in two fold cores. Raster connectivity is only an approximation—one geological fault may be split and crossing faults may merge—so **these results are spatial-block results, not verified whole-segment cross-validation**. This additional limitation keeps every scale research-only and must be addressed in any future confirmatory protocol. The diagnostic receipt is [`label_fold_component_audit_20261006.json`](../receipts/label_fold_component_audit_20261006.json).

## Gravity-resolution audit

The pinned feature mirror is 100 m, but 100 m pixels are not evidence of 100 m independent gravity observations. In a systematic two-pixel decimation of the valid footprint, `iso_grav_anom` lag-1 Pearson correlation is **0.999794 east–west / 0.999862 north–south** at 100 m; at 1 km it is **0.980458 / 0.987034**. Full lag series and the raw band tags are in [`input_spacing_audit_20261006.json`](../receipts/input_spacing_audit_20261006.json).

USGS DS 234 says Nevada isostatic gravity was derived from complete Bouguer gravity; its companion Bouguer page states that the statewide product was compiled from 71,055 gravity-station measurements and gridded at 1 km. In this review, the official INGENIOUS GDR 1391 page's associated USGS regional-gravity release README was also read directly; it describes `GB_iso_grav_anom.tif` as a 1 km cell-size grid. The mirror's exact source/version and native station coordinates are not exposed, so these official sources are scale warnings, **not proof that the challenge band is that exact grid**. The empirical autocorrelation measures smoothness of the rasterized mirror and cannot recover the native station spacing. The 500 m Gaussian σ (1.177 km FWHM) was the preregistered primary choice; native spacing remains unresolved.
